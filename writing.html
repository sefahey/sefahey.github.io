Research notes authored while working in the FDIC's Chicago Region (spanning Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Michigan, Ohio, and Wisconsin). Summaries of each article are compiled below. Full articles available upon request via email.

Oil Price Shock May Negatively Affect the Auto Industry, Especially the Detroit 3 FDIC Hot Topics · May 2026

The U.S. auto industry enjoyed several years of strong profitability following the pandemic as prices surged due to supply shortages and strong demand. However, profits fell as support for the once-burgeoning electric vehicle market was withdrawn and global trade headwinds picked up. The outbreak of conflict in the Middle East caused a sharp rise in oil prices, adding another stressor to the sector. While factors like an increasingly affluent buyer base, lower inventories, and relatively low incentives could alleviate revenue and cost pressures, much higher commodity prices are a key risk for the auto sector. The Detroit 3 are particularly at risk due to their concentration in producing larger and more expensive vehicles.

Chicago Region Employment Growth Is Largely Dependent on a Small Group of Metros FDIC Hot Topics · July 2025

The Chicago Region has surpassed its pre-pandemic employment peak, but nearly half of the Region’s Metropolitan Statistical Areas (MSAs) are still below their pre-pandemic peaks. A small group of high-growth metros have driven the Region’s recovery. These MSAs are more exposed to sectors like the transportation and utilities sector that have grown strongly during this expansion, while waning MSAs are more exposed to the manufacturing sector that has struggled in recent years. Growth in the transportation and utilities sector is now faltering. The Region relies more on its high-growth MSAs than the nation does, and a correction in those metros could lead to substantial weakening of the Region’s labor market.

Recent Legislative Changes Have Contributed to Growth in Ohio's Energy Production FDIC Hot Topics · July 2024

Following a change in state legislation in 2023, the Ohio Oil and Gas Land Management Commission (OGLMC) began awarding bids to energy developers for exploration on state controlled land. Advances in horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing (fracking) had already propelled the state to become the nation’s sixth-largest natural gas producer and twelfth-largest crude oil producer. While growth in energy production has not increased employment and gross state product as was forecast, tax revenues have risen in mining-related areas and counties heavily affiliated with the energy sector. These effects and other demographic and economic trends in mining-dependent counties may affect bank deposit and loan growth patterns.

Automotive Industry Strikes to Have Short-Term but Noticeable Economic Impact FDIC Hot Topics · October 2023

On September 15, the United Auto Workers (UAW) began a partial strike against the Detroit Three auto manufacturers after their contracts expired. A targeted strike that began at three assembly plants has widened to six assembly plants and thirty-eight parts distribution centers. As a result of the strikes, other workers in auto manufacturing and related industries have been laid off. Though reductions in both employment and production should be temporary, both the national economy and some regional economies could experience repercussions in the following quarters, with the economies of some metros particularly at risk. The impact on the banking sector is likely to be limited.

Temporary Help Services Contraction Could Spell Trouble for Chicago Region Employment FDIC Hot Topics · July 2023

Growth in the Chicago Region’s professional and business services employment is slowing despite continuing growth nationally. Declines in temporary help services, an industry that makes up a large portion of professional and business services, are viewed as a leading indicator of employment that turns negative months before total employment. While other leading indicators display mixed signs for the Chicago Region’s economy, the decline in temporary help services may be an indicator of a looming regional recession.
